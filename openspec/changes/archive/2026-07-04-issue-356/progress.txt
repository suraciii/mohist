## Codebase Patterns

- Time injection convention: `private readonly TimeProvider _time` field; two
  constructors — public one hard-codes `TimeProvider.System`, internal one
  accepts `TimeProvider time` as the trailing parameter. Reference:
  `AttachmentService.cs:36` (public/public pair) and `SystemUpdateService.cs`
  (public/internal pair) for this issue.
- Production TimeProvider registration: `services.TryAddSingleton(TimeProvider.System);`
  is already at `MohistServiceRegistration.cs:89` and `MohistSiloRegistration.cs:55`.
  No DI change is needed for services that hard-code the default.
- Test fixture for clock control: `Microsoft.Extensions.Time.Testing.FakeTimeProvider`
  (already in `Mohist.Server.Tests.csproj:18`) — construct with a baseline
  `DateTimeOffset`, then `.Advance(TimeSpan)` or `.SetUtcNow(DateTimeOffset)`
  to drive transitions. Reference: `InboxSubscriptionStoreSpecs.SetAsync_SecondWrite_UpdatesUpdatedAt`.

## Decisions / Gotchas

- Two constructor pattern: chosen to match `AttachmentService` (public/public
  pair) and adapt to the existing `SystemUpdateService` (public/internal
  pair). The internal constructor's signature is preserved at the start
  (existing positional params unchanged), with `TimeProvider` appended as the
  last parameter to minimize call-site churn.
- De-staticized helpers: `CreateFailedTransition` and `ApplyTransitionLog`
  were `private static` and never pure (read wall clock internally). Losing
  `static` imposes no real loss of testability or reasoning — call sites
  already invoked them without a class qualifier.
- Helper signature compatibility: `CreateService` and `CreateConsistencyService`
  keep their existing `static SystemUpdateService` return shape to avoid
  breaking 40+ call sites; new overloads expose the `FakeTimeProvider` via a
  tuple for the two new specs that drive clock-dependent transitions. The
  discarded fake clock defaults to a fixed baseline
  (`2026-06-30T00:00:00Z`) so behavior is deterministic even when the test
  doesn't read the clock back.
- Source-audit fallback: kept the legacy `private static` lookup as a
  fallback so the audit tests stay green if someone later re-staticizes the
  helpers — defensive against future refactors.
